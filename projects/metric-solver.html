    The rules

    • Split the grid into states. Each state is a connected group of squares with at least one rotation or reflection symmetry.
    • If every symmetry of a state fixes the same single square, that square is the state's capitol.
    • Stepping between two neighbouring squares costs the size of the smaller of their two states.
    • A clue is the cheapest total cost from that square to any capitol. A 0 marks a capitol.
    • The answer is the sum, over all rows, of the square of the row's total state size.

    How the solver works

    1. Each square starts with a size range for its state. Clues narrow those ranges. For example, neighbours whose costs differ by 16 must both sit in states of 16 or more squares.
    2. For every open square it lists each symmetric shape of up to 12 squares that could contain it, plus the option "part of a bigger state".
    3. It commits at the square with the fewest options left. It then recomputes cost bounds for the whole board and backs up as soon as any clue can't be met.
    4. Big states are resolved last, from whatever region is left over. The finished grid is checked against every clue from scratch.
